# MOTRA Market Analysis

**Last Updated:** March 6, 2026

## The Core Problem

Autonomous vehicles (robotaxis) operate 24/7 with no driver to notice spills, trash, or maintenance issues between rides. As fleets scale to millions of rides per week, **who cleans and services these vehicles?**

Current solutions are fragmented:
- In-house depot operations (expensive, doesn't scale)
- Traditional car washes (not designed for rapid turnaround — and damages sensors)
- No mobile, on-demand infrastructure exists

## How Robotaxi Depots Work Today

AV companies operate "fleet operations centers" (depots) staffed like airline ground ops. **Each 100-vehicle fleet requires 80-120 operations staff** across 7 roles:

1. **Fleet Operations Manager** — scheduling, availability, coordination
2. **AV Technicians** — sensor calibration, LiDAR, compute hardware (specialized)
3. **Fleet Maintenance Technicians** — tires, brakes, suspension, HVAC, battery
4. **Vehicle Readiness / Turnaround Team** — cleaning, charging, inspections, staging
5. **Remote Assistance Operators** — tele-guidance for unusual driving situations
6. **Dispatch & Fleet Monitoring** — routing, health monitoring, demand management
7. **Safety & Incident Response** — accidents, passenger issues, law enforcement

**The misconception:** Robotaxis eliminate human labor.
**The reality:** They shift labor from drivers → fleet operations and technical support.

## How AV Companies Handle It Today

**Waymo's Current Approach:**
- **Hand wash only** — Can't use automatic car washes (destroys sensors)
- **Depot-based** — Vehicles return to facilities for cleaning/charging
- **Outsources to Transdev** and other third-party contractors
- **40+ workers at one facility** servicing hundreds of vehicles/day
- **Known depots:** 201 Toland St, SF; 737 Harrison St, SF
- **Honor system for messes** — Passengers self-report, pay cleaning fees

**Pain Points:**
- Depot = downtime = lost revenue ($30-40/hr per idle vehicle)
- Hand washing at scale = labor intensive
- No solution for mid-day cleaning needs in the field
- Sensor cleaning is critical but specialized
- 80-120 staff per 100 vehicles = massive fixed cost

## The Gig Economy Model

### Why It Works for MOTRA

1. **Variable demand** — AV cleaning needs spike at certain times
2. **Geographic distribution** — Vehicles are everywhere, not centralized
3. **Low barrier to entry** — Cleaning + basic maintenance doesn't require special certs
4. **Existing workforce** — Detailers, mobile mechanics, gig workers
5. **Proven model** — Uber/DoorDash proved gig dispatch at scale

### MOTRA Tech Workers

**For Gig Workers ("MOTRA Techs"):**
- Mobile app for job dispatch
- Training/certification program (AV-specific: sensor handling, EV safety)
- Earnings dashboard
- Standardized equipment kits (cleaning supplies, basic tools)

**For AV Fleet Operators:**
- Dashboard for fleet-wide scheduling
- Real-time tracking of all services
- Quality metrics and reporting
- Reduction in depot dependency and fixed costs
