## 📋 PHASE 2: GET YOUR FIRST 5 RECOMMENDATIONS (Week 1-2)

Recommendations (called "Faves") are EVERYTHING on Nextdoor. Your business won't even show up in neighbor searches until you have at least 1. Five or more = 30% higher engagement on all posts.

### Who to Ask (Priority Order):
1. **Your current 4-5 recurring customers** — they already love you, easy yes
2. **Any past one-time customers** — even if they didn't continue service
3. **Friends/family in STL County who are on Nextdoor** — they can vouch for you as a person/business owner
4. **Other local businesses you've worked with** — vets, pet stores, etc.

### Recommendation Coaching
Tell customers what to mention (don't script it, just suggest):
- Reliability (shows up when expected)
- Thoroughness (yard is spotless after)
- Easy to work with
- Good price / fair value
- Trustworthy (you trust them in your yard)

**Goal:** 5 recommendations in the first 2 weeks. This puts you ahead of EVERY competitor on Nextdoor in STL.

## 📋 PHASE 4: THE PERSONAL ACCOUNT HUSTLE (Ongoing — 10 min/day)

This is where the REAL magic happens. Your business posts are limited to 2/month, but your personal account has no limits.

### Daily Routine (10 minutes)
1. **Open Nextdoor** → Scroll the feed
2. **Look for recommendation requests** — "Anyone know a good..." posts about ANY pet service, lawn care, or home services
3. **Reply helpfully** — even when it's not about poop scooping
4. **Engage naturally** — comment on neighborhood stuff, be a real neighbor

### How to Respond to "Looking for..." Posts

**When someone asks about pet waste removal directly:**
```
"Hey! I run Tidy Tails — I do weekly pet waste removal all around [area]. $70/month flat rate, no contracts, and I don't charge extra for multiple dogs. Happy to give you a free quote — just shoot me a message! 🐕"
```

**When someone asks about pet services generally:**
```
"If you need a great [vet/groomer/dog walker], I've heard good things about [name]. And if you ever need someone to handle the less glamorous side of pet ownership (yard cleanup 😅), I'm your guy — Tidy Tails Pet Waste Removal. We're local and no-contract."
```

**When someone complains about dog waste in their neighborhood:**
```
"Ugh, that's the worst. If it helps, I run a pet waste removal service (Tidy Tails) and we also do one-time cleanups for situations like this. Some HOAs hire us for common areas too. Happy to help if you need it!"
```

### The 80/20 Rule
- **80% of your posts** should be helpful, neighborly, non-promotional (recommending restaurants, helping someone find a plumber, commenting on community events)
- **20% of your posts** can mention your business

People recommend businesses from people they like. Be likeable first, business owner second.

### Topics to Engage With (As TJ, the Neighbor)
- Lost/found pets → "Hope you find them! Check [local shelter]..."
- New to the neighborhood posts → "Welcome! Great area for dogs..."
- Home improvement questions → Share what you know
- Local restaurant recommendations → Everyone loves these threads
- Weather complaints → "At least the dogs are happy..."
- Neighborhood safety → Be a concerned neighbor
- Pet-related anything → Your wheelhouse, chime in naturally

## 🚫 WHAT NOT TO DO

1. **Don't spam.** If every post is "hire me!", neighbors will report you
2. **Don't argue with negative comments.** Respond professionally, take it offline
3. **Don't post from your business page only.** The personal account engagement is what drives trust
4. **Don't ignore messages.** Nextdoor tracks response time — fast responses rank higher
5. **Don't pay for sponsorship before proving organic works.** Free first, paid to scale
6. **Don't use stock photos.** Real photos of real work. Nextdoor users can smell corporate BS
7. **Don't crosspost identical content from Facebook/Instagram.** Write Nextdoor-native content
