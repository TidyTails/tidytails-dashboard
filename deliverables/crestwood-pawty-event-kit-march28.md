## 🗣️ CONVERSATION SCRIPTS

### The Opener (casual, not salesy)
Don't lead with a pitch. Lead with the dog.

> **"Hey, what a beautiful [breed]! Just adopted today? Congrats!"**  
> *(let them talk about their dog for 30 seconds — people LOVE this)*
>
> **"I'm TJ — I run Tidy Tails, a pet waste cleanup service here in Crestwood. Just wanted to say hey and give you my card in case you ever need help keeping the yard clean. Most of my customers are people who just got tired of doing it themselves."**

### The Transition (when they seem interested)
> **"Yeah, so basically I come by once a week — or every other week if you want — and clean up everything. Most yards take me about 10 minutes. You never have to think about it again."**
>
> **"Starting at $15 a visit, and right now I'm doing first cleanup free for new customers. Just to let you see how it works."**

### The Close (get the number)
> **"Hey, let me grab your number real quick and I'll shoot you a text this week with pricing for your area. No pressure at all — just so you have it when you're ready."**
>
> OR: **"Here's my card — text me anytime. I'll send you a quick quote in like 2 minutes."**

### Objection: "I can just do it myself"
> **"Oh totally, most people do! I did too before I started this. But I'll tell you — the folks who switch to a service? They always say the same thing: 'Why didn't I do this sooner?' It's like mowing — you CAN do it, but it's way nicer when someone else handles it."**

### Objection: "That seems expensive"
> **"Totally get it. Most customers pay about $60-70 a month — less than a gym membership or a couple takeout dinners. And your yard stays clean every single week without you thinking about it."**

### Objection: "I only have one small dog"
> **"You'd be surprised — I have a few customers with just one dog. It still adds up faster than you think, especially in spring when it rains and you can't get out there. And honestly, the small-dog yards are my fastest jobs."**

## 🤝 VET CLINIC RELATIONSHIP — THE BIGGER PLAY

The vet hosting this event is potentially worth 5-15 customers over the next year. Here's how to approach them:

### Before the Event
- Look up the clinic name, main vet's name
- Check if they have a bulletin board or "local services" area

### At the Event — Talk to the Vet/Staff
> **"Hey, I'm TJ with Tidy Tails — we do pet waste removal here in Crestwood. I just wanted to say thanks for hosting this event, it's awesome."**
>
> *(chat for a minute, then:)*
>
> **"I'd love to leave some business cards or flyers here if you have a community board. A lot of my customers are new pet owners who hear about me through their vet. I can also leave some for your front desk if that's cool?"**
>
> **"And if you ever have clients ask about yard cleanup, feel free to send them my way. I'd be happy to do the same — send dog owners to you guys for their vet care."**

### After the Event (Monday follow-up)
Send the clinic a text or email:
> **"Hey [Vet Name/Clinic], this is TJ from Tidy Tails. Just wanted to say thanks again for the awesome Paw-ty on Saturday! I met some great people. If it's cool with you, I'd love to drop off a stack of business cards for your front desk. Happy to return the favor — I always recommend a good vet to my customers. Have a great week!"**

## 💡 PRO TIPS

1. **Remember dog names, not just people names.** "How's Buddy doing?" hits way harder than "Hey, uh, we met Saturday."
2. **Take a selfie with dogs** (ask permission). Post to social media. Tag the event/vet if possible.
3. **Don't sit behind a table the whole time.** Walk around. The leads are in conversation, not at your booth.
4. **Wear something with the Tidy Tails name** if at all possible. Even a handwritten name tag: "TJ — Tidy Tails 🐾"
5. **Bring a notepad.** Jot down dog names + owner details right after each conversation. Your memory will fail you by Saturday night.
