## 🎯 Recommended: Tiered Pricing Structure

### New Pricing Matrix (For New Customers)

| | Weekly | Biweekly | Monthly |
|---|--------|----------|---------|
| **1 Dog** | $22/visit ($88/mo) | $40/visit ($80/mo) | $70 |
| **2 Dogs** | $27/visit ($108/mo) | $48/visit ($96/mo) | $85 |
| **3 Dogs** | $32/visit ($128/mo) | $55/visit ($110/mo) | $100 |
| **4+ Dogs** | $37/visit ($148/mo) | $60/visit ($120/mo) | $115 |
| **Large Yard Add-on** | +$5/visit | +$5/visit | +$10/mo |

### Why This Works
1. **$88/month base** is right at industry average — competitive but not cheap
2. **Per-dog surcharge** ($5/extra dog) matches Doggie Doodies' model and reflects real labor
3. **Monthly option stays at $70** — keeps the affordable entry point for budget customers
4. **Large yard add-on** compensates for extra time on properties over ~5,000 sq ft
5. **Still undercuts Doggie Doodies** on most tiers by 5-10%
6. **Way above Scoop Soldiers** ($15/wk) — lets TJ compete on quality, not price

### One-Time / Initial Clean Pricing

| Yard Condition | Price |
|----------------|-------|
| Light (1-2 weeks buildup) | $75 |
| Moderate (2-4 weeks) | $100 |
| Heavy (1-3 months) | $150 |
| Disaster (3+ months) | $200+ (quote) |

**Current:** $100 flat for all. This leaves money on the table for heavy jobs and is too expensive for light ones.

## 🛡️ How to Implement Without Losing Customers

### Phase 1: New Customers (Immediate — Zero Risk)
- Update website pricing page to reflect tiered structure
- Update all ad copy and directory listings
- Use new pricing for every new quote going forward
- **NO changes for existing customers yet**

### Phase 2: Existing Customer Adjustment (60-90 Days Out)
Send a personal text to each customer. Here's the template:

> **For customers getting a modest increase ($5-10/visit):**  
> "Hey [Name]! Quick heads up — I'm adjusting my pricing to [new rate] starting [date, 30 days out]. It's the first increase since you signed up, and I wanted to let you know personally. You've been awesome and I appreciate your business! 🐕"

> **For Mrs. Candee (significant increase):**  
> "Hey Mrs. Candee! I wanted to reach out personally. When we started, I set your rate at $15/visit as a launch price while I was getting established. I'll need to adjust to $20/visit starting [date, 30 days out] — still below what anyone else charges in the area. You've been one of my first customers and I really appreciate you! 🐕"

### Psychological Tips:
1. **Frame as "adjustment" not "increase"** — sounds less aggressive
2. **Give 30 days notice** — shows respect
3. **Personalize every message** — no mass texts
4. **Anchor against competitors** — "still below what most charge"
5. **For extreme underpricing (Mrs. Candee), raise in 2 steps** — $15 → $20 now, $20 → $22 in 6 months
6. **Never apologize for pricing** — you provide a real service with real value
7. **Expect to lose 0-1 customers** — industry data shows <5% churn from reasonable increases

### Phase 3: Annual Increases (Every Spring)
- 5-10% annual increase is industry standard
- Swoop Scoop (7-figure company) does 10% annually
- Time it with spring (peak demand = less resistance)
- Same personal text approach
