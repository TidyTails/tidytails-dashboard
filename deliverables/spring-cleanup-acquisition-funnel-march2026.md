### Why $75 Base (Not $50, Not $100)
- **$50 is too cheap** — you're already the premium player in STL. Don't race to the bottom. Spring cleanups are labor-intensive (soggy, compacted, mixed with leaves).
- **$100+ scares away first-timers** — the goal isn't to maximize revenue per cleanup. It's to GET THEM IN THE DOOR for recurring.
- **$75 is the sweet spot** — covers ~1 hour of work, feels fair to customers, leaves room for "upsell discount" to recurring.

### The Conversion Play: "Save $25"
When quoting a spring cleanup, ALWAYS present both options:

> *"Spring cleanup is $75. But here's what most people do — sign up for weekly service at $25/week, and I'll do the spring cleanup for FREE as your first visit. You save $50 and your yard stays clean all season."*

## PART 4: SPRING RUSH OPERATIONS PREP

### What You Need Before Marketing Kicks In

**Supplies (Stock Up This Week):**
- [ ] Extra 5-gallon buckets (2-3 more than usual)
- [ ] Heavy-duty trash bags (spring cleanups = 2-3x normal bag volume)
- [ ] Rubber boots or waterproof shoes (spring yards are WET)
- [ ] Hand sanitizer / wet wipes
- [ ] Rake for leaf/poop combo piles (common in spring)
- [ ] Business cards or door hangers to leave at neighboring houses

**Scheduling:**
- Block out 60-90 min per spring cleanup (vs. 15-20 min for regular visits)
- Schedule cleanups on separate days from regular route if possible
- Or batch them: "Spring Cleanup Saturdays" — knock out 3-4 in a day

**Leave-Behind Marketing:**
After each spring cleanup, leave a door hanger at 3-5 neighboring houses:

> *"Your neighbor just got their yard cleaned up for spring 🌱 Want yours done too? Spring cleanup special: $75 (or FREE when you sign up for weekly service). Text Darius: 636-354-7406"*

This is called **route density marketing** — it's how Swoop Scoop built clusters of customers on the same street. Cheaper to service, easier to grow.
