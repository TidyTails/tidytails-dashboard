📅 7-Day Spring Referral Blitz — Execution Plan

Follow this day-by-day. Total time commitment: ~30 minutes.

Day 1 (TODAY — Friday)
Text 2-3 of your favorite customers. Use Template 1 (Casual Ask) or Template 4 (Humor). Don't text all 5 at once — save some for next week.
Time: 5 minutes
Day 2 (Saturday)
Post on Instagram. Use Caption 2 (Real Talk) — it's the most authentic and works well on weekends when people are scrolling.
Time: 3 minutes
Day 3 (Sunday — Scooping Day)
Take before/after photos at 2-3 yards. Post one as an Instagram Story. Mention the referral deal verbally to customers you see in person — the in-person ask is 5x more effective than text.
Time: 5 minutes (photos happen while working)
Day 4 (Monday)
Create Nextdoor Business Page at business.nextdoor.com (if not already). Post the Introduction (Post 1). This is a one-time setup.
Time: 10 minutes
Day 5 (Tuesday)
Text remaining 2-3 customers. Use Template 2 (Spring Hook) — mention their dog by name if you know it. Also post Referral Promo (Caption 3) on Instagram.
Time: 5 minutes
Day 6 (Wednesday)
Drop FB group posts. Use the Tag-a-Friend post in 2-3 local groups. Also post Nextdoor Spring Cleanup Special (Post 2).
Time: 5 minutes
Day 7 (Thursday)
Follow up. If any customers said "let me think of someone" earlier in the week, text them: "Hey, any luck thinking of someone who needs yard help? No worries if not!" Keep it light.
Time: 3 minutes
💡 Total Time: ~35 minutes across the entire week. That's 5 minutes a day to potentially add 2-3 customers worth $210/month in recurring revenue.

🚫 What NOT to Do

  • ✗ Don't text all 5 customers on the same day — spread it out, feels more natural
  • ✗ Don't follow up more than once if they don't respond — nobody likes being pressured
  • ✗ Don't make the referral the focus of every customer interaction — service quality first
  • ✗ Don't post the same content on Nextdoor and Facebook — different audiences, different tone
  • ✗ Don't skip the in-person ask on Sunday — it converts 5x better than text
  • ✗ Don't offer more than $25 credit — keeps margins healthy and still feels generous
