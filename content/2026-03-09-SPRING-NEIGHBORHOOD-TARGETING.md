## The Strategy

Not all neighborhoods are equal. The ideal Tidy Tails customer lives in a suburb with:
- **Median HH income $75K+** (willing to pay for convenience)

- **Dog parks nearby** (indicates high dog ownership density)

St. Louis ranked **#6 most pet-friendly city in the US** (WalletHub). That's your market.

## 🔑 KEY INSIGHTS

### Why Kirkwood-Webster-Ballwin?
1. **Geographic cluster** — All within 15-minute drive of each other
2. **Route density** — Multiple customers per trip = more profit per hour
3. **Income sweet spot** — $110K-$121K median means they CAN afford it
4. **Family demographics** — Families with yards and dogs and no time
5. **Less franchise competition** — POOP 911 focuses on Olivette/Chesterfield

### The HOA Angle (Especially Ballwin)
HOA communities are GOLD for pet waste removal because:
- Board can mandate waste cleanup → creates demand you don't have to create
- One sale to the board = 10-50 customers at once
- Recurring revenue on autopilot
- Professional image matters more (favors local businesses over DIY)

### Spring Is Your Season
- Winter waste thaw creates VISIBLE, URGENT problem
- People spending more time outdoors = noticing the mess
- Spring cleaning mindset = "let's fix everything"
- New dog adoptions spike in spring → new customers who need you
- Run "Spring Cleanup Special" — first deep clean at discount, then recurring

### Route Efficiency Math
If all your customers are in Kirkwood-Webster-Ballwin:
- 15 customers × 15 min each = 3.75 hours of actual work
- 5 min drive between stops = 1.25 hours of driving
- **Total: 5 hours for 15 customers** vs. 7+ hours if scattered across county
- That's $300-450 in revenue for a half day. Not bad.
