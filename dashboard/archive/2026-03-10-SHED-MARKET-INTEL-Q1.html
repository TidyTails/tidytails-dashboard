📊 Key Pricing Takeaways

Market sweet spot: $3,000–$6,000 for standard sheds. This is where 80% of FB Marketplace buyers land. TJ should anchor ads in this range.
TuffShed's Home Depot pricing is transparent: Anyone can look up $4,998 for a 10x12 Tahoe. TJ's WoodMaster listings should be positioned 10-20% below for same-quality builds to win the "price conscious but quality aware" buyer.
Rent-to-own is EVERYWHERE: Cardinal, STL Shed Guy, Cook, 5-Star — all offer RTO with no credit check. If TuffShed/WoodMaster offers RTO, TJ MUST mention it in every ad. If not, this is a major disadvantage.
Amish-built is a magic word: STL Sheds, Classic Buildings, and others all lead with "Amish-built." It communicates quality without saying a word about specs. If WoodMaster has Amish connections, highlight it.

📝 Winning Marketplace Ad Formula (Based on Top Performers)

The pattern that wins: Competitor analysis shows the best-performing shed Marketplace listings follow this structure:
  1. Price anchor in title: "$XX/month" or "Starting at $X,XXX"
  2. Social proof fast: "★★★★★ rated" or "100+ sheds delivered in STL"
  3. Remove friction: "No credit check" / "Free delivery" / "Installed for you"
  4. Urgency: "Spring special" / "Only 3 at this price" / "This week only"
  5. CTA: "Message me for a free quote — I respond in under 5 minutes"

🆚 What TJ's Competitors Are Doing Right

  • Cardinal Buildings: Incredible copywriting — funny, relatable, zero corporate jargon. "Built Like a Tank. Delivered Like Pizza." stands out.
  • STL Shed Guy: Leading with "1st Month Down" in every headline — immediately answers "can I afford this?"
  • STL Sheds (Ben): Personal service emphasis — customers literally came back for a 2nd shed. Name-drops owner.
  • 5-Star Buildings: SEO-heavy approach — city-specific landing pages (Chesterfield, Fenton). Captures Google traffic.

🚫 What They're Doing Wrong (TJ's Opportunity)

  • Most dealers have NO video content — no walkthroughs, no build process, no customer testimonials. VIDEO WINS on Marketplace.
  • Slow response times — most "contact us for a quote" = 24-48hr wait. Speed kills in Marketplace.
  • No personality — most listings read like a spec sheet. Cardinal is the exception.
  • No "why me" — nobody explains why to buy from THEM vs Home Depot vs anyone else.
  • Missing before/after — empty yard → shed installed. Transformation content crushes generic product photos.

⚡ TJ's Competitive Advantages

✅ What TJ Has That Others Don't

  • Works at TuffShed: Insider knowledge of product quality, build process, common issues. Can speak to durability from EXPERIENCE, not a brochure.
  • Speed: Can respond to Marketplace messages instantly from phone. Most dealers take 24-48hrs.
  • Product depth: Sells TuffShed AND WoodMaster — two brands, more options. Most dealers represent one line.
  • Personal service: "You deal with ME, not a rotating sales team." Same person from first message to delivery day.
  • Local expertise: Knows which sheds pass HOA in which STL neighborhoods. Invaluable for suburbia.
  • No lot overhead: No showroom rent = can price more competitively on margin.

❌ Where TJ's Vulnerable

  • No showroom: Can't say "come walk through one." Competitors with lots (Cardinal, 5-Star, Cook) have physical advantage.
  • Rent-to-own: If TuffShed/WoodMaster doesn't offer easy RTO, TJ loses price-sensitive buyers to Cardinal/STL Shed Guy.
  • Brand recognition: "Darius" (TJ's shed persona) doesn't have an established brand — competing against 40yr-old Cook and nationally known TuffShed.
  • No Google presence: backyardbuildingsstl.com exists but likely not ranking for shed terms. Competitors own SEO.
  • Time constraint: Day job at TuffShed + Tidy Tails + shed side hustle = limited posting bandwidth.

🎯 Positioning Strategy: "Your Shed Guy"

The winning position: TJ shouldn't try to be a "shed company." He should be "your shed guy" — the knowledgeable friend in the industry who saves you from overpaying, undersizing, or buying the wrong thing. Think car dealer insider who helps friends get the right deal.

💡 Spring Strategy (March–May)

Spring is shed season. Every competitor is ramping up. The difference between closing 2 deals and 10 deals this spring comes down to:
  1. Consistency: Post 3-5 Marketplace listings per week. Different angles, same CTA.
  2. Speed: First to respond wins. Period. 5-minute response time or less.
  3. Video: Nobody in STL is doing shed walkthrough videos on Marketplace. Be first.
  4. Expertise: Position every conversation as a consultation, not a sale. "What's your yard like? What do you need to store? Let me figure out the right fit."
  5. Follow-up: 80% of shed buyers don't buy on first contact. Text 3 days later: "Hey, still thinking about that shed? Happy to answer any questions."
