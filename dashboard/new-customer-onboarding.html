When someone says "yes" — even a soft yes — follow this exact flow. Speed is the job. The faster they're in the system, the more likely they stick.
⚡ The 5-Step Close Sequence
1
Confirm the "Yes" Out Loud
Say: "Awesome — let me get you set up right now. Takes 2 minutes."
Don't wait. Don't say "I'll have someone reach out." Do it immediately. If they have a dog in their arms, you have their full attention for exactly 90 seconds.
⏱ Do this within 10 seconds of hearing "yes"
2
Collect the 3 Must-Haves
Only ask these three, in order:
① Name — "What's your name?"
② Phone — "Best number for texts?" (almost everyone texts)
③ Address or neighborhood — "And roughly what area are you in?"

Everything else can come via text. Don't overwhelm them at the table.
⏱ Takes 60 seconds. Stop at 3.
3
Send the Confirmation Text Immediately
Pull out your phone. Send the Confirmation Text (Tab ③) while they're still standing there.
They see the text arrive — this locks it in psychologically. Now it's real to them.
⏱ Send before they walk away
4
Quote Pricing (Confidently, No Apology)
Say the price out loud. Don't trail off. Don't add "but it depends."
1 dog, weekly = $35/mo. 2 dogs = $45/mo. One-time = $75-100.
If they pause: "We do a flat rate — no surprises on the bill, ever."
⏱ Say it once, clearly, then move on
5
Set the First Service Date
Don't leave it open-ended. Suggest a day: "We can get you on the schedule for [specific day]. Does that work?"
If they say "I need to check" → "No worries — I'll follow up in that text I just sent."
Log them in the Follow-Up Engine as "Warm" if no date is set.
⏱ Always leave with a date OR a follow-up plan
🧠 Psychology Notes
⚡
The 48-Hour Window
Interest drops ~60% per day after an event. A "yes" at 10 AM Saturday is worth $0 by Monday night if you haven't texted. Send the confirmation text IN PERSON.
💬
Text > Call
90% of people in this demographic prefer texts to calls. Always start with text. Offer to call only if they ask or if 2 texts go unanswered.
🐕
Use the Dog's Name
If you learn their dog's name at the Paw-ty, use it in the first text. "Can't wait to keep Biscuit's yard clean!" — this makes it personal and memorable.

💡 Scheduling Rules of Thumb
📍
Cluster by Geography
Add customers to the route day that matches their area. Driving 30 min between stops kills your hourly rate. Keep routes tight.
📅
Offer Specific Days, Not Open-Ended
Don't say "what day works?" — say "We're in your area on Tuesdays, does that work?" Customers with open choices ghost.
⚡
First Service Within 1 Week
Don't let the first service slip past 7 days from signup. The longer the gap, the higher the cancel risk. If their preferred day is 10+ days out, offer the sooner route day instead.
🕐
Morning vs Afternoon Ask
Some people work from home and don't want strangers in the yard mid-call. Ask: "Morning (8–11 AM) or afternoon (1–4 PM)?" Then stick to it.

💬 Close Objections — Handle & Move On
❓ "I need to think about it."
Totally fine! "Here's what I'll do — I'll shoot you a text in a day or two. If it's not for you, just let me know and no hard feelings. Deal?" Then send the confirmation text anyway. Most people don't say no when they've committed to a soft yes.

Key: Lower the commitment threshold. Make it easy to say "yes for now."
❓ "It seems a little expensive."
"Here's a way to think about it — you're probably spending $8–10 at Starbucks a couple times a week, right? This is literally less than that, and you get your yard back every single week. Plus first visit's on me."

If still hesitant: offer biweekly ($25/mo) as a starting point.
❓ "My dog is small — there's not much to pick up."
"Totally hear you — but even little dogs do their thing every single day. And honestly, it's about the yard as much as the amount. Nobody wants to think about it at all. That's the whole point." Then show the biweekly pricing.
❓ "I can just do it myself."
"100% — a lot of people say that. Our best customers said the same thing before they started. The truth is it just never happens consistently. When the weather sucks, when life gets busy — the yard wins. We're the insurance against that."
❓ "Can I cancel if I need to?"
"Absolutely. No contracts, ever." Just text us and we'll stop. That's it. No cancellation fee, no notice period. We keep customers by doing a great job, not by trapping them.
❓ "I want to ask my spouse / partner first."
"Of course! I'll text you the info right now so you have it handy when you talk. Sound good?" → Send confirmation text. Gives them something tangible to show their partner. Moves it forward without pressure.
❓ "Do you serve [their neighborhood]?"
"Yep! We cover all of St. Louis County and surrounding areas. What's your neighborhood?" — then confirm. We're mobile. Don't say no unless it's truly 40+ miles out.
❓ "How do I know you're legit?"
"Totally fair question. We're a local St. Louis business — here's our website: tidytailsstl.com. We also have Google reviews you can check. We've been in business for [X time] and all our customers know my personal cell." — hand them your card or show the site on your phone.
🎯 The Universal Close After Any Objection
Use after handling any objection
"Here's what — let me grab your number and shoot you a quick text with the info right now. No pressure, and if it's not the right time, totally fine. But this way you've got everything and can decide on your own timeline. Sound good?"
This works because it: lowers the commitment (just a text), keeps momentum (you're doing something), and gets their number in your phone regardless.
