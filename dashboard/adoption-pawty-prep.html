💬 Conversation Scripts (5 Scenarios)

›

🎯 Scenario 1: They Just Adopted (THE MONEY SHOT)

They're walking out with a new dog, beaming. This is your moment.

"Congrats! Oh my god, what a beautiful dog. What's the name? [Let them gush for 10 seconds — they want to.] Hey, quick thought — the yard situation gets real pretty fast with a new pup. I do weekly yard cleanups for folks around here. First visit's free for new adopters today, and I've got a deal going — $35 a month for the first three months. Here's my card. Seriously, future you will thank you."

🎯 Scenario 2: Browsing / Not Sure Yet

They're looking at dogs but haven't committed. Plant the seed.

"These dogs are amazing, right? [Chat about the dogs for a sec.] Hey, if you end up taking one home — I run a pet waste removal service. Most people don't think about it until week three when the yard's a minefield. Here, take my card. I do a free first cleanup for anyone who adopts."

🎯 Scenario 3: They Already Have a Dog

They came to look or brought their own dog along. Existing dog = existing poop problem.

"Oh, who's this? [Pet their dog.] Beautiful. How long have you had [him/her]? ... So you know the yard drill then. [Laugh.] I actually do weekly yard cleanups — like a lawn service but for the... other stuff. Most of my customers tell me it's the best $70 they spend every month. You in [city]? Here, take one of these."

🎯 Scenario 4: Talking to Open Door Volunteers

Build the referral relationship. These people talk to adopters ALL the time.

"Hey, thanks for doing this — seriously, Open Door is amazing. I run Tidy Tails, a pet waste removal service here in STL. A lot of new adopters don't think about yard maintenance until it's a problem. Would you mind mentioning us to folks when they adopt? I give every new adopter a free first visit. Here's a stack of cards — happy to leave some at your facility too."

🎯 Scenario 5: Talking to Hometown Vet Staff

The BIG play. Vet clinics see every dog owner in the area. One referral partner = steady leads forever.

"Hey, I really appreciate you hosting this — great event. I'm Jamie with Tidy Tails, we do pet waste removal all across St. Louis County. I was thinking — a lot of your clients probably deal with the yard situation, especially with multiple dogs. Would you be open to us leaving some cards in your lobby? We could do a referral thing too — anyone you send our way gets a free first cleanup, and we'd be happy to refer clients to you guys for vet care. Win-win?"

💡 General Rules

  • Lead with the dog, not the pitch. Always ask about their dog first. People love talking about their pets.
  • Don't be salesy. You're a guy at a dog event who happens to solve a real problem. Casual > corporate.
  • Get their number. A card handed out is a 5% callback. A phone number captured is a 40% conversion. Always try to get the number.
  • Use humor. "The stuff nobody wants to talk about" or "the dirty work" — it breaks the ice. Everyone laughs about poop.
