💡 When to Use This
If they push back via text, send a short version of the response below. Don't paste the whole script — pick the key 1–2 sentences. Match their energy. Keep it friendly.
"I need to think about it"
"Totally fair! Can I ask — is there a specific concern I can help answer? Sometimes it's just the pricing or the logistics, and those are easy to work through."
💡 "Think about it" almost always means one real objection hiding under it. Ask the question.
"It's too expensive"
"Totally get it! Just to put it in perspective — at $35/month biweekly, that's less than $9/week, or about the cost of one coffee. And you never have to step in anything again. Worth one free visit to see if it makes sense?"
💡 Coffee math always works. The first visit is FREE — that eliminates all risk.
"I only have one small dog — not that much poop"
"Haha right! Honestly even small dogs produce more than you'd think over a week — and it's still about not wanting to deal with it. The biweekly rate for one dog is only $35/mo. First visit free so you can just try it."
💡 Don't argue. Validate, then give the number and the free visit.
"What if I need to cancel?"
"No contracts, cancel anytime! Literally just text me and we stop — no fees, no awkward conversations. We keep customers because they like us, not because we trap them."
💡 The "trap them" line shows you're confident in your service quality. It usually gets a laugh.
"My yard is really big"
"Gotcha — we charge by dogs, not yard size, so a bigger yard doesn't change the price. One dog is one dog. Our guys are fast."
💡 This is actually a strength. Don't get defensive — just state it clearly.
"My kids usually do it"
"Ha — how's that actually going? 😄 We hear that one a lot. The thing is, we show up every single visit regardless of weather, homework, or summer travel. Guaranteed clean yard every week."
💡 Don't push too hard. The light joke usually makes them laugh because they know their kids are unreliable.
"I'm not sure about timing / just moved in"
"That makes total sense! Save my number and just text me when you're ready. The Paw-ty offer is good until April 15, so there's still time. No rush at all."
💡 Forcing timing pushes them away. Give the deadline without pressure and let them come back.
"How do I know you're legit / reliable?"
"Totally fair question! We're local (based in St. Louis County), been doing this for about a year, and all our reviews are from real customers you can reach. Want me to send you a couple references you can text directly?"
💡 Offer real customer references — most won't take you up on it, but the offer builds immediate trust.
