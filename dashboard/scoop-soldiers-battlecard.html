  • Multi-dog households: TJ's flat $70/mo covers the yard regardless. Scoop Soldiers charges per visit and may upcharge for extra dogs

🎯 Their Weaknesses — TJ's Opportunities

Every Goliath has gaps. Here's where we beat them:

1. 🏠 Franchise = Impersonal

They're a franchise operator following a playbook. TJ IS Tidy Tails. When customers call, they get TJ (or Jamie). Not a call center. Not a franchisee running 3 other businesses. The personal touch is TJ's #1 weapon.

  • Franchise tech turnover is high (low-wage, high-churn industry)
  • Customer gets a different person each time with franchises
  • TJ can build real relationships — "hey, noticed Buddy seems slower this week, everything ok?"

2. 🐌 Response Time

Their quote flow: Zip code → form → wait for local franchisee to call back. TJ can respond in minutes, not hours. In service businesses, speed to lead = money.

  • National toll-free routes to a queue, then to local operator
  • Franchisee may not respond same-day (or even next-day)
  • TJ picks up the phone or texts back immediately

3. 💰 Franchise Overhead = Margin Pressure

Franchisees pay: initial fee ($25-50K est), ongoing royalties (6-8% of revenue), marketing fund (1-2%), plus their own local costs. That overhead either gets passed to customers or squeezes service quality.

  • TJ keeps 100% of revenue
  • Can undercut on price if needed while maintaining margins
  • Can reinvest in quality instead of corporate fees

4. 📍 Early-Stage STL Presence

Their STL footprint is tiny. Only 2 confirmed suburb pages. No local reviews. No established reputation in STL specifically. TJ's existing customers, GBP reviews, and Nextdoor presence all matter MORE than their national brand right now.

5. 🎯 No Local SEO Depth

  • Their Missouri page only shows KC cities
  • Generic template content on STL pages (not locally written)
  • No "Kirkwood," "Webster Groves," "Crestwood" pages = opportunity
  • TJ's Google Business Profile with real STL reviews outranks a national template page

6. 🤖 Template Everything

Their military branding is corny to some. "Land mines," "troops," "battle" — it's heavy-handed. TJ's authentic, laid-back brand (real dude, real dog lover, local) resonates differently with STL customers who prefer genuine over gimmicky.

🗣️ Battle Scripts

When customers mention Scoop Soldiers or ask about competitors, here's how to respond. Never trash-talk. Position, don't attack.

Script 1: "I was also looking at Scoop Soldiers..."

TJ's Response
"Yeah, I know them — they're a big national franchise. Great that you're doing your homework! Here's the thing though: when you call Tidy Tails, you get me. I'm the owner. I'm the one in your yard every week. You're not getting a rotating cast of minimum-wage employees."
"I also live right here in [neighborhood]. If something ever comes up, I'm a text away and I can usually swing by same-day. With a franchise, you're calling a 1-800 number and hoping someone gets back to you."
"And honestly? Our pricing is competitive. Especially if you've got multiple dogs — we don't nickel-and-dime per dog."

Script 2: "They're cheaper than you..."

TJ's Response
"I hear ya. Their per-visit rate might look lower, but let me ask — how many dogs you got? Because our $70/month covers the whole yard, no matter how many pups. With them, extra dogs can push the price up."
"Also worth knowing: they're a franchise, so there's a lot of overhead baked into that price that doesn't benefit you. With me, every dollar goes into doing the job right. No franchise fees, no corporate cut."
"Tell you what — try me for a month. If it's not the best service you've had, I'll refund your money. No contract, no hassle."

Script 3: "Why should I pick a small local company?"

TJ's Response
"Because this isn't a side gig for me — this is my business, my reputation, my livelihood. When I'm in your yard, I treat it like it's my own. I notice things. I know your dogs' names. I'll text you if I see something off."
"With a franchise, you're a number in a system. With Tidy Tails, you're a neighbor I'm taking care of. That's the difference."
"Plus, you're supporting a local St. Louis business, not sending profits to corporate Texas."

Script 4: Jamie's Commercial Pitch (vs Scoop Soldiers)

Jamie's Commercial Script
"Hi, I'm Jamie with Tidy Tails — we're a local St. Louis pet waste removal company. I know there are national franchises out there now, but here's what makes us different:"
"Same tech every visit — your residents will recognize us and feel comfortable. We're not rotating random employees through your property."
"Local owner available — if there's ever an issue, you're not calling an 800 number. TJ, our owner, is right here in [area]. Problems get solved fast."
"Competitive pricing without franchise markup — because we don't pay franchise fees, we can be more flexible on pricing and custom plans."
"Can I set up a quick walkthrough this week? Takes 10 minutes and I'll have a custom quote for you same day."

⚠️ What NOT To Say

  • ❌ Never say "they're bad" or "they suck" — makes you look small
  • ❌ Never say "they're a franchise" like it's a dirty word — just note the difference in experience
  • ❌ Never directly name-drop Scoop Soldiers unless the customer brought them up first
  • ❌ Never compete on price alone — compete on experience, reliability, personal touch
  • ✅ Always redirect to YOUR strengths, not their weaknesses
