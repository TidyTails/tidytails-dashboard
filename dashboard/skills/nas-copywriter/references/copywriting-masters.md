# Copywriting Masters: Detailed Techniques

## Classic Five

### 1. Claude Hopkins (1866-1932)

**Core Philosophy:** "Advertising is salesmanship in print."

**Key Techniques:**

**Test Campaigns as Decision Engine**
- Never guess when you can test
- Small tests settle disputes that opinions cannot
- Track responses with coupons, codes, unique URLs

**Specificity Beats Generality**
- "Reduced 25%" > "reduced significantly"
- Precise claims imply testing and truthfulness
- Quantified claims carry more weight

**Couponing for Attribution**
- Track exactly where leads come from
- Every campaign should be measurable
- If you can't measure it, you can't improve it

**Sample Applications:**
- Use specific numbers: "Saves 3.5 hours per week"
- Always include trackable response mechanisms
- A/B test headlines before full rollout

---

### 2. David Ogilvy (1911-1999)

**Core Philosophy:** "The consumer is not a moron, she is your wife."

**Key Techniques:**

**Research-Forward Copy**
- Study the product exhaustively before writing
- Know the customer better than they know themselves
- Facts are more persuasive than adjectives

**Headline Craft**
- 5x more people read headlines than body copy
- Include the benefit and brand in the headline
- Specific beats general: "At 60 miles an hour..."

**Factual Specificity**
- Objective observations beat hyperbole
- Sensory details create believability
- High-status benefits expressed as facts

**Story Appeal and Curiosity**
- "The man in the Hathaway shirt" (eyepatch)
- Visual hooks that demand explanation
- Intrigue that pulls readers into body copy

**Sample Applications:**
- Write 10 headlines before choosing one
- Include at least one specific fact/number
- Respect reader intelligence—don't dumb it down

---

### 3. John Caples (1900-1990)

**Core Philosophy:** Test everything. Self-interest headlines win.

**Key Techniques:**

**Self-Interest Headlines**
- "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano"
- Put the reader's benefit in the headline
- Appeal selection is the decisive variable

**Direct Response Structure**
- Hook → Proof → Offer → Call to Action
- Every element serves the response
- Remove anything that doesn't drive action

**Testing Culture**
- Change one variable at a time
- Headline tests yield biggest gains
- Let data, not opinion, guide decisions

**The 4 U's (Caples-influenced):**
- Useful (benefit to reader)
- Urgent (reason to act now)
- Unique (differentiated)
- Ultra-specific (concrete details)

**Sample Applications:**
- Test headline variations first (biggest leverage)
- Always lead with reader benefit
- Include clear, specific call to action

---

### 4. Eugene Schwartz (1927-1995)

**Core Philosophy:** "Copy cannot create desire. It can only channel existing desire."

**Key Techniques:**

**Market Awareness Levels**
1. **Most Aware** — Know you, want to buy → lead with offer
2. **Product Aware** — Know product, not convinced → lead with proof
3. **Solution Aware** — Know solutions exist, not yours → lead with uniqueness
4. **Problem Aware** — Feel the pain, don't know solutions → lead with problem
5. **Unaware** — Don't know they have a problem → lead with story/curiosity

**Market Sophistication Levels**
- Level 1: Be first, make the claim
- Level 2: Enlarge the claim
- Level 3: Add mechanism ("how it works")
- Level 4: Add proof and specificity
- Level 5: Identification (story, personality)

**Headline Intensity**
- Direct mail demands powerful first impression
- The headline does 80% of the work
- Match headline intensity to market sophistication

**Sample Applications:**
- Diagnose awareness level before writing
- Adjust copy sophistication to market maturity
- Use mechanism explanations in competitive markets

---

### 5. Bill Bernbach (1911-1982)

**Core Philosophy:** "The truth isn't the truth until people believe you."

**Key Techniques:**

**Relevance + Creativity**
- Creativity must serve the selling idea
- Reject both dullness AND showy irrelevance
- Every creative choice must connect to the product truth

**Respect Audience Intelligence**
- Don't insult with obvious manipulation
- Trust them to "get it"
- Subtlety can be more persuasive than hammer blows

**Truth as Persuasion**
- "We try harder" (Avis—admitting #2 position)
- Honesty creates trust
- Vulnerabilities can become strengths

**Anti-Formula Rigidity**
- Rules are starting points, not prisons
- Fresh approach beats tired formulas
- The idea matters more than the structure

**Sample Applications:**
- Find the genuine truth about the product/service
- Turn weaknesses into positioning strengths
- Don't follow formulas blindly—think first

## Modern Five

### 6. Gary Halbert (1938-2007)

**Core Philosophy:** "The list is more important than the copy."

**Key Techniques:**

**Market-First Discipline**
- A starving crowd beats brilliant copy
- Right offer to right list > perfect words to wrong people
- Research the market before writing a word

**The A-Pile Secret**
- Make your mail look like personal correspondence
- Avoid "advertising look" in direct mail
- Hand-addressed envelopes outperform labels

**Letter Structure**
- Grabber opening (curiosity or benefit)
- Story that demonstrates proof
- Irresistible offer
- Risk reversal (guarantee)
- Urgency (real deadline)
- P.S. (often read first—put key benefit here)

**Sample Applications:**
- Spend more time on targeting than copy
- Use P.S. for your strongest benefit or deadline
- Make offers so good they feel like a "no-brainer"

---

### 7. Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers)

**Core Philosophy:** "Copy is a conversation with the customer, using their words."

**Key Techniques:**

**Voice of Customer Mining**
- Pull language directly from reviews, interviews, surveys
- Use their exact words—don't paraphrase
- Real customer language > copywriter language

**Conversion Copy Testing**
- Controlled experiments, not opinions
- Statistical significance before conclusions
- Iterate based on data, not gut

**Specificity in Landing Pages**
- One page = one offer = one action
- Remove all friction and distraction
- Match message to traffic source

**Sample Applications:**
- Mine Amazon reviews for customer language
- Run A/B tests on headlines and CTAs
- Use customer quotes as headlines (with permission)

---

### 8. Ann Handley

**Core Philosophy:** "Write like a human, not like a brand."

**Key Techniques:**

**Reader Respect**
- You're a guest in their inbox/feed
- Deliver value before asking for anything
- Write to one person, not "users"

**Scalable Writing Habits**
- Consistency beats perfection
- Show up regularly
- Edit ruthlessly—shorter is usually better

**Content Marketing Discipline**
- Useful > promotional
- Build trust over time
- Teaching = selling (soft sell)

**Sample Applications:**
- Write as if to one specific person
- Ask "Would I read this if I weren't paid to?"
- Cut 20% after first draft

---

### 9. Neville Medhora (Kopywriting Kourse)

**Core Philosophy:** "Write like you talk."

**Key Techniques:**

**Practical Formulas**
- AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)
- PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)
- Before-After-Bridge

**Conversational Style**
- Short sentences. Like this.
- Read your copy aloud
- If you wouldn't say it, don't write it

**Reusable Checklists**
- Swipe files for inspiration
- Templates speed up production
- But always customize to the specific situation

**Sample Applications:**
- Record yourself explaining the product, transcribe, edit
- Use contractions (you're, it's, don't)
- Break up walls of text

---

### 10. Dan Kennedy

**Core Philosophy:** "Whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer, wins."

**Key Techniques:**

**Sequencing**
- Follow-up is where money is made
- Multi-step campaigns beat one-shots
- Different messages for different stages

**Curiosity Hooks**
- Open loops that demand closure
- "What happens next" pulls readers forward
- Tease before reveal

**Narrative Selling**
- Stories > bullet points for engagement
- Customer transformation stories
- Before/after structure

**Urgency Without Desperation**
- Real deadlines, not fake ones
- Scarcity that's believable
- Reason WHY there's urgency

**Sample Applications:**
- Plan 5-7 touch sequence, not just one message
- Use "you'll discover..." curiosity language
- Tell customer success stories in detail
