# Positioning Angles Framework

## Core Definitions

**Positioning:** "The act of designing a company's offering and image to occupy a distinctive place in the minds of the target customers." (Kotler)

**Value Proposition vs. Positioning:**
- Value proposition = whole cluster of benefits and costs
- Positioning = the FEW key benefits that create advantage
- Confusion leads to "everything-and-the-kitchen-sink" messaging

## The Three-Part Structure

Every strong position requires:

### 1. Frame of Reference (FOR)
What category do customers put you in? What alternatives do they compare you to?

- **Category membership** — What market are you in?
- **Competitive set** — Who do you want to win against?
- **Context/occasion** — What situation do you "own"?

### 2. Points of Difference (POD)
What makes you better/different within that frame?

**Three tests for a valid POD:**
- **Desirability** — Do customers actually want this?
- **Deliverability** — Can you actually provide it?
- **Differentiability** — Can competitors NOT easily match it?

### 3. Points of Parity (POP)
What must you match to be considered at all?

- Category basics customers expect
- You must "break even" on these to be considered
- PODs alone aren't enough if you fail on POPs

## The Six Positioning Angle Families

### 1. Performance/Attribute Leadership

**What it emphasizes:** A small set of functional advantages (quality, speed, durability, etc.)

**When it works:**
- Advantages are provable and durable
- You can demonstrate superiority
- Technical audiences

**Key risk:** Easily copied; can become feature arms race

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- POD test (desirability/deliverability/differentiability)
- Comprehension studies
- Perceived uniqueness scores

**Copy approach:** Lead with the specific claim + proof

**Example:** "60 miles an hour... electric clock" (Rolls-Royce)

---

### 2. Parity-First, Then Differentiating

**What it emphasizes:** Meeting all "break-even" expectations, then one standout benefit

**When it works:**
- High category standards
- Buyers screen on basics before considering extras
- B2B RFP situations

**Key risk:** Messaging sounds generic if parity dominates

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- POP adequacy checks
- Consider/shortlist rate

**Copy approach:** Establish credibility on basics, then reveal the "plus"

**Example:** "We do everything they do. And this."

---

### 3. Emotional Promise Anchored to Attributes

**What it emphasizes:** An emotional benefit tightly linked to product realities

**When it works:**
- Cluttered category
- Emotion can drive preference
- Functional parity among competitors

**Key risk:** "Brand can't live on emotions alone" without attribute linkage

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- Emotional resonance + attribution to concrete drivers
- Brand tracking/consideration lift

**Copy approach:** Lead with feeling, support with facts

**Example:** Dove "Real Beauty" — confidence (emotional) backed by real women/real moisturization (attribute)

---

### 4. Segment-Owned (Narrow Beachhead)

**What it emphasizes:** Owning one priority segment with tailored proof

**When it works:**
- Focus beats breadth
- Early growth stages
- Underserved niche exists

**Key risk:** TAM anxiety; internal pressure to broaden too soon

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- Segment-level conversion
- Retention
- Repeat purchase frequency

**Copy approach:** "Built specifically for [segment]"

**Example:** Tidy Tails for busy St. Louis dog owners (not "everyone with a yard")

---

### 5. Transparency/Trust Reset

**What it emphasizes:** Trust, honesty, improvement, and "why believe us"

**When it works:**
- Credibility is low
- Industry has trust issues
- Reputation repair needed

**Key risk:** If execution doesn't match, backlash is amplified

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- Sentiment
- Customer satisfaction/retention
- Sales lift with lower promo dependence

**Copy approach:** Acknowledge the problem, show the change, prove with action

**Example:** Domino's "Our pizza was bad. We fixed it." (+9.9% same-store sales)

---

### 6. Price/Value Equation

**What it emphasizes:** Lower cost, better deal, or superior economics

**When it works:**
- Economics dominate choice
- Your costs are structurally lower
- Price-sensitive segment

**Key risk:** Commoditization, margin erosion, race to bottom

**Best tests/KPIs:**
- Price premium/elasticity tests
- Profit-impact modeling

**Copy approach:** Make the math obvious

**Example:** "That's less than your streaming subscriptions" (shed financing)

## Selecting the Right Angle

### Step 1: Understand Jobs-to-Be-Done
- What primary outcome does the customer hire this product for?
- Use their language, not internal feature terms
- Primary needs > tertiary implementation details

### Step 2: Map Your Value Proposition
Define the full cluster of benefits, then narrow to the few that matter most.

### Step 3: Apply the POD Tests
For each potential point of difference:
- Is it **desirable** to customers?
- Can we **deliver** it consistently?
- Is it **differentiable** from competitors?

### Step 4: Verify Points of Parity
What category basics must we match to be considered at all?

### Step 5: Choose the Angle Family
Based on your situation:
- Market maturity
- Competitor landscape
- Your actual strengths
- Customer sophistication

### Step 6: Craft the Positioning Statement

**Template:**
For [target customer] who [needs/wants X], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [point of difference] because [reasons to believe].

**Tidy Tails Example:**
For busy St. Louis dog owners who want a clean yard without the hassle, Tidy Tails is the local pet waste removal service that's reliable, detail-oriented, and run by genuine dog people—unlike national franchises that treat you like a number.

**TuffShed Example:**
For St. Louis homeowners tired of garage clutter, TuffShed/WoodMaster is the shed dealer that makes ownership easy with flexible financing, no-credit-check options, and free delivery—so you can get organized without financial stress.

## Applying to Copy

Once positioning is clear, every piece of copy should:

1. **Reinforce the frame of reference** — Remind them what category you're in
2. **Establish parity** — If needed, show you meet category basics
3. **Hammer the POD** — Make your key difference memorable
4. **Provide reasons to believe** — Proof, not just claims

The positioning angle dictates:
- What headline approach to use
- What proof to emphasize
- What emotional tone fits
- What CTA makes sense
