# Slide Framework Reference

Detailed prompts and examples for each of the 10 Sequoia slides.

---

## Slide 1: Company Purpose

**Goal:** Define the company/business in a single declarative sentence.

**Prompts:**
- What do you do in one sentence?
- If you had to explain this to a stranger in an elevator, what would you say?
- Complete: "We help [WHO] [DO WHAT] by [HOW]"

**Formula:**
```
[Company] is a [category] that [value prop] for [target customer].
```

**Examples:**
- "Airbnb is a trusted community marketplace for people to list, discover, and book unique accommodations around the world."
- "Stripe is a technology company that builds economic infrastructure for the internet."
- "Tidy Tails is a pet waste removal service that keeps St. Louis yards clean and dog-friendly."

**Red Flags:**
- Uses "and" more than once (too broad)
- Over 25 words (too complex)
- Contains jargon or buzzwords
- Doesn't mention the customer

## Slide 2: Problem

**Goal:** Describe the pain and current workarounds.

**Prompts:**
- What pain does your customer experience?
- How do they currently solve this problem?
- What's broken about the current solution?
- What does this pain cost them (time, money, frustration)?

**Structure:**
1. State the problem clearly
2. Quantify the pain if possible
3. Explain current workarounds
4. Show why workarounds fail

**Example (Tidy Tails):**
- **Problem:** Homeowners with dogs face yards full of pet waste
- **Current workaround:** DIY cleanup on weekends
- **Why it fails:** Time-consuming, unpleasant, often skipped → yard becomes unusable
- **Pain:** Kids can't play, hosting embarrassing, health hazard

**Red Flags:**
- Founder-centric problem ("I was frustrated...")
- Solution in disguise (describing what you do, not the pain)
- Unquantified pain

## Slide 3: Solution

**Goal:** Show your value proposition and how it makes life better.

**Prompts:**
- How does your solution eliminate the pain?
- Where does your product/service fit in the customer's life?
- What are 2-3 concrete use cases?
- What's the "before vs. after" for your customer?

**Structure:**
1. Value proposition (1-2 sentences)
2. How/where it fits
3. 2-3 use cases

**Example:**
- **Value prop:** "Weekly professional cleanup means you never touch poop again"
- **Fit:** Recurring service, scheduled around your life
- **Use cases:** 
  - Busy professional: saves 2+ hours/week
  - Family with kids: safe play area guaranteed
  - Elderly homeowner: physical task eliminated

**Red Flags:**
- Feature list without benefits
- No clear "before vs. after"
- Solution doesn't map to stated problem

## Slide 4: Why Now

**Goal:** Explain the timing — why is this the right moment?

**Prompts:**
- What recent changes make this possible or necessary?
- What trends are you riding?
- What's different now vs. 5 years ago?
- Why hasn't this been solved before?

**Categories of "Why Now":**
- **Technology shift** — New tech enables your solution
- **Regulatory change** — Laws/rules changed
- **Behavioral shift** — People do things differently now
- **Market timing** — Demographics, economics changed
- **Competitive gap** — Incumbents asleep or dying

**Example:**
- "Pet ownership hit record highs post-COVID (70% of US households)"
- "Dual-income households have less time than ever"
- "Gig economy normalized outsourcing personal tasks"

**Red Flags:**
- No concrete data or dates
- "Why Now" could've been said 10 years ago
- Trends are speculative, not proven

## Slide 5: Market Size

**Goal:** Show the market is big enough to matter.

**Prompts:**
- Who exactly is your target customer? (Be specific)
- What's your TAM (Total Addressable Market)?
- What's your SAM (Serviceable Available Market)?
- What's your SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)?

**Definitions:**
- **TAM** — Total market demand if you had 100% share (top-down)
- **SAM** — Portion you can actually serve (geography, segment)
- **SOM** — Realistic capture in 3-5 years (bottoms-up calculation)

**Calculation Pattern:**
```
TAM: [# of potential customers] × [annual value per customer]
SAM: TAM × [% you can actually reach]
SOM: SAM × [realistic market share %]
```

**Example (Local Service Business):**
- TAM: 500K dog-owning households in Missouri × $1,200/year = $600M
- SAM: 150K households in St. Louis metro × $1,200 = $180M
- SOM: 1% penetration in Year 3 = $1.8M

**Red Flags:**
- Citing analyst reports without connection to your business
- "$1 trillion market" for a local service business
- No bottoms-up calculation

## Slide 6: Competition

**Goal:** Show you understand the landscape and can win.

**Prompts:**
- Who are your direct competitors?
- Who are indirect competitors (alternatives)?
- What are your competitive advantages?
- Why will you win against each?

**Framework:**
| Competitor | What they do | Their weakness | Your advantage |
|------------|--------------|----------------|----------------|

**Always include:**
- Direct competitors (same solution)
- Indirect competitors (different solution, same problem)
- Status quo ("do nothing" or DIY)

**Example:**
| Competitor | Weakness | Our Advantage |
|------------|----------|---------------|
| National franchise | Impersonal, high prices | Local, premium service, relationship |
| Local competitor | Poor reliability | On-time guarantee, communication |
| DIY | Time-consuming, unpleasant | "Never touch it again" value prop |

**Red Flags:**
- "We have no competition" (never true)
- Only listing direct competitors
- Advantages are features, not positioning

## Slide 7: Product

**Goal:** Show what you're actually selling.

**Prompts:**
- What's your product/service lineup?
- What features matter most to customers?
- What's your tech/IP advantage (if any)?
- What's on the roadmap?

**Structure:**
1. Core offering (what you sell today)
2. Key features/differentiators
3. IP or moat (if applicable)
4. Development roadmap

**For Service Businesses:**
- Service tiers and pricing
- Delivery method
- Quality guarantees
- Expansion services

**Example:**
- **Core:** Weekly pet waste removal ($25/week)
- **Add-ons:** Biweekly ($45), monthly ($70), one-time deep clean ($100)
- **Differentiator:** Same-day service, photo confirmation, deodorizing included
- **Roadmap:** Dog walking add-on, pet-sitting referrals

**Red Flags:**
- Feature soup without hierarchy
- No clear "hero" product
- Roadmap is unfocused wishlist

## Slide 8: Business Model

**Goal:** Show how you make money and unit economics work.

**Prompts:**
- What's your revenue model?
- What's your pricing and why?
- What's customer lifetime value (LTV)?
- How do you acquire customers (CAC)?
- What's LTV:CAC ratio?

**Key Metrics:**
- **Revenue model:** Subscription, transaction, freemium, etc.
- **Pricing:** What you charge and price anchoring
- **LTV:** Revenue per customer × average lifespan
- **CAC:** Cost to acquire one customer
- **LTV:CAC:** Should be >3:1

**Example:**
- **Revenue:** Recurring subscription (weekly/biweekly/monthly)
- **Pricing:** $25/week = $100/month average
- **LTV:** $100/month × 18 months = $1,800
- **CAC:** $50 (Facebook ads + referral bonus)
- **LTV:CAC:** 36:1 🔥

**Red Flags:**
- No unit economics
- LTV:CAC <3:1 without explanation
- Revenue model doesn't match product

## Slide 9: Team

**Goal:** Show why YOU can execute this.

**Prompts:**
- Who are the founders? Relevant experience?
- What's your unfair advantage?
- Who are key team members or advisors?
- What gaps exist and how will you fill them?

**What VCs Look For:**
- **Founder-market fit:** Why are YOU the one to solve this?
- **Relevant wins:** Past successes, even small ones
- **Complementary skills:** Technical + business, etc.
- **Grit indicators:** Evidence of persistence

**Structure:**
1. Founders (name, role, relevant background)
2. Key team members
3. Advisors/board (if applicable)
4. Notable gaps and hiring plan

**Example:**
- **TJ Halpin, Founder:** 5 years in home services sales (TuffShed), built Tidy Tails to 30 customers in 8 months
- **Advisory:** Local business owners, marketing consultant
- **Hiring plan:** Operations manager at $10K MRR

**Red Flags:**
- "Passionate entrepreneur" (meaningless)
- No relevant experience
- Solo founder with no plan for gaps

## Slide 10: Financials

**Goal:** Show the numbers and the ask.

**Prompts:**
- What are your current financials (revenue, costs)?
- What are your projections (12-36 months)?
- What are key assumptions?
- How much are you raising and for what?

**Components:**
1. **Current state:** Revenue, MRR, customers, burn
2. **Projections:** Revenue forecast with assumptions
3. **Use of funds:** Where the money goes
4. **The ask:** Amount + terms (if applicable)

**Projection Guidelines:**
- 12-month: Conservative, bottoms-up
- 24-month: Optimistic but defensible
- 36-month: Vision (acknowledge uncertainty)

**Example:**
- **Current:** $500 MRR, 5 customers, break-even
- **12-month:** $3K MRR (30 customers)
- **Use of funds:** Marketing ($2K), equipment ($1K), part-time help ($2K)
- **The ask:** Not raising — self-funded growth

**Red Flags:**
- Hockey stick with no explanation
- Projections disconnected from customer acquisition plan
- "We'll figure out monetization later"

## Appendix: Slide Design Tips

**Text:**
- Max 6 lines per slide
- Max 6 words per line
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs

**Visuals:**
- One idea per slide
- Use charts/graphs for data
- Consistent color scheme

**Flow:**
- Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → Ask
- Each slide should make them want the next slide
